Chess opening plans for iPhone and iPad

Stop memorizing. Start understanding.

Learn the fight, not the tree.

Afterbook shows you what an opening is fighting for: the squares that matter, the pieces that do the work, and the pawn break that decides the middlegame. Short lessons. Quizzes that ask "what is the break?", not "what is move 14?"

The painted board

See the fight before the moves.

Every lesson step shows one idea on the board. Contested squares glow, and each one carries a count of who attacks it, so "d4 must wait" is something you can see, not something you have to trust.

  • Gold and solid is your plan.
  • Clay and dashed is your opponent's.
  • One idea per step, in a few short sentences.

Board lenses

Read the position like a coach.

Switch lenses to look at the same position a different way. Structure draws pawn chains from base to head and marks half-open files. Squares finds holes and outposts. Lines, King, and Tension show open files, the king's shelter, and pawns about to trade.

  • Look from your side, your opponent's, or both.
  • Fixed rules on the position. No engine, no score.

Drills

Drill the idea, not move 14.

Quizzes ask what a strong player actually thinks about: tap the square both sides want, name the break, pick the critical piece, choose the plan. Get it right and the board paints the answer.

  • Spaced review on ideas, so they come back right before you forget them.
  • Quick 5 fits in three minutes a day.

Structure library

Every opening has a structure.

Sixteen pawn structures you will meet again and again: the Carlsbad, the isolated queen's pawn, the French chain, the Hedgehog, and more. Each one shows its plans for both sides and the openings that reach it.

Learn a structure once and you already know the plan in several openings.

Compare structures

One pawn changes the plan.

Put two structures side by side and Afterbook points at the pawns that differ, then shows what that does to each file and to the tension in the center. The Carlsbad and the Slav triangle look alike; the plans do not.
